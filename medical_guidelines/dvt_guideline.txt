DVT - diagnosis and treatment of deep vein thrombosis

Deep-vein thrombosis is a common condition that can lead to complications such as postphlebitic syndrome, pulmonary embolism, and death. Here is an algorithmic strategy combining pretest probability, D-dimer testing, and compression ultrasound imaging that allows safe investigation of suspected thrombosis. 

FIRST STAGE - A Clinical Model for predicting pretest probability of deep vein thrombosis DVT
A score of 2 or higher indicates the probability of DVT is likely; it is unlikely
 
The Clinical. characteristics that are measured:
-Active cancer, either an ongoing treatment or administered within the previous 6 months, or palliative, yields one point
-Paralysis, paresis, or recent plaster immobilization of the lower extremities gives one point
-If the patient was recently bedridden for more than 3 days or had a major surgery within the previous 12 weeks requiring general or regional anesthesia, the patient gets 1 point
- Localized tenderness along the distribution of the deep venous system yields one point
- Swelling of the entire leg: one point
- Swelling of the calf more than 3 cm larger than the asymptomatic side (measured 10 cm below the tibial tuberosity) gives one point
-Pitting edema confined to the symptomatic leg gives one point
-One point for collateral superficial veins (nonvaricose)
- Previously documented DVT yields one point
- If the patient gives an alternative diagnosis at least as likely as DVT, the patient gets MINUS TWO POINTS

SECOND STAGE - The Algorithm

* Compression ultrasonography is the imaging test of choice. A lack of compressibility of a venous segment indicates DVT is present, while a normally compressible segment indicates no DVT.

First, the pretest probability of DVT is determined by the first stage, based on which the DVT can be unlikely (probability score less than or equal to one) or likely probability score greater than one. In both cases, the next phase is the D/dimer test

IF DVT UNLIKELY:
	•	If D-Dimer negative: DVT ruled out, no imaging needed.
	•	If D-Dimer positive: Ultrasound imaging is performed
                    -  If ultrasound is noncompressible (positive), then the patient is treated with anticoagulation therapy.
                    -  If ultrasound is compressible (negative), DVT is ruled out.


IF DVT IS LIKELY:
A D-dimer test is done in both cases; even if the D-dimer test is positive or negative, ultrasound imaging is done. (D-dimer alone cannot exclude DVT at high pretest probability)

	•	If the D-dimer test is negative and the ultrasound is compressible, DVT is ruled out
	•	If the D-dimer test is negative and the ultrasound is noncompressible, treat the patient with anticoagulation therapy
	•	
	•	If the D-dimer test is positive and the ultrasound is noncompressible, treat the patient with anticoagulation therapy.
	•	If the D-dimer test is positive and the ultrasound is compressible, repeat the ultrasound in 1 week.
	•	                - If the repeat ultrasound is noncompressible, treat the patient with anticoagulation therapy
	•	                - Repeat ultrasound is compressible; rule out DVT
